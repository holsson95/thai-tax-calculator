Bringing money into Thailand?

Under Revenue Department Order Por. 161/2566 (as amended by Por. 162/2566), foreign income earned on or after 1 January 2024 in a year you were a Thai tax resident (180+ days) may be taxable when you bring it into Thailand, whether in that year or any later year. Income earned before 2024 and the original capital of savings or investments are treated differently, so what you remit and when it was earned matter.

Thai personal income tax, the essentials

Thailand uses a progressive personal income tax system with eight brackets, ranging from exempt on the first 150,000 THB to 35% on income above 5,000,000 THB. Tax residents (those who spend 180 or more days in Thailand in a calendar year) must report income earned in Thailand and any foreign income remitted into the country. Non-residents are taxed only on Thai-sourced income. The annual filing deadline is 31 March each year for paper returns. Online filers have had an 8-day extension in past years, but it is not yet confirmed for the 2026 tax year.

Three numbers to know

Residency
180 days

Spend 180 or more days in Thailand in a calendar year and you become a Thai tax resident, liable to declare all Thai-source income and remitted foreign income.

Filing deadline
31 March

PND 90 (freelancers & multiple income sources) and PND 91 (salaried employees) are both due by 31 March for the prior tax year (paper filing). The 8-day online-filing extension granted in past years is not yet confirmed for this tax year.

Filing threshold
120,000 THB

Commonly cited threshold: single filers with assessable income above 120,000 THB per year must file a return, rising to 220,000 THB for married couples filing jointly. We have not yet verified these figures against a primary source.
